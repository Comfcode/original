---
name: Border advice verification
description: How to handle incomplete or internally ambiguous official travel guidance without inventing certainty.
---

Do not infer that an old border-entry exception remains usable when the same official notice also gives a past vehicle-stay deadline. Record the unresolved relationship and require current agency clarification rather than promising entry or inventing a new blanket ban.

**Why:** Official Lithuanian BY-plate guidance combined narrow entry exceptions with an already-past stay deadline, without clearly explaining how they interact for later journeys. Repeating only the exceptions would conceal a material risk to travellers.

**How to apply:** Re-check current primary guidance when revising either language or the checker. Distinguish the date editors inspected a source, the source's own publication/effective dates, and actual travel-day checkpoint operation. An accessible official page is not agency approval of this guide.

Verify the document content, not just an official domain and HTTP success. A customs news URL without its trailing slash can return a sitemap; an EAEU act-record URL can return the catalogue instead of the act. Prefer the agency's substantive annex PDFs and inspect their amendment headers separately rather than calling an older annex a complete current consolidation.

**Why:** Both behaviours obscured otherwise available primary customs law and could make a source check appear complete when the relevant text was never read.

**How to apply:** Read the actual operative table/paragraph, its amendment coverage and any later amendment annex before claiming a rule is confirmed. Treat secondary databases as corroboration, not authentic publication.
