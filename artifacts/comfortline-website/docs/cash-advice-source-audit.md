# Cash article source audit

Editorial inspection: 16 October 2026. Scope: the RU/EN article with Russian slug `вывоз-60-евро-в-беларусь-из-литвы-латвии`. The publication date and both canonical slugs are retained; the correction has its own modification date.

## Conclusions

- **EU legislation:** Article 1za of Regulation (EC) No 765/2006 prohibits banknotes in any official member-state currency, not just euros. It contains **no €60 amount**. The personal-use exception is conditional on necessity for travellers or accompanying immediate family, not a universal allowance.
- **Latvian guidance:** VID's Cash page says “only in reasonable amounts for personal travel needs or to meet diplomatic requirements.” It does not state €60. This is national explanatory guidance applying the exception, not a separate uniform numerical EU limit.
- **Lithuanian verification gap:** The current sanctions overview and cash guidance reviewed do not establish a current €60 allowance. Historical press reports are not sufficient evidence of a present national rule. Do not assert either a guaranteed €60 allowance or that Lithuania has formally abolished such a practice. Obtain current customs clarification for a particular journey.
- **Passenger accounts supplied by the site owner:** The owner reports €60 as common actual border practice. The RU/EN article and FAQ label this explicitly as passenger-reported practice, not independently verified customs policy or legislation. No dated accounts or checkpoint-specific evidence were supplied, so universal applicability and guaranteed clearance are not claimed. This does not mean there are no legal banknote restrictions.
- **Declarations:** The EU external-border threshold is **€10,000 or more**, including exactly €10,000, calculated across currencies for the actual carrier. Declaration is not authorisation for an otherwise prohibited export.
- **Currencies:** Polish złoty, Czech koruna and Swedish krona are covered. USD is outside this particular banknote provision, not automatically unrestricted.
- **Enforcement:** No support was established for “usually not confiscated”, universal administrative-only liability, or predictions about checkpoint shifts. These claims were removed rather than replaced with invented penalties.

## Primary evidence

1. [EUR-Lex: Regulation 765/2006, consolidation 24 July 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02006R0765-20260724), Article 1za(1)–(2). The [original act landing page](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/765/oj/eng) identified this as the current consolidation when inspected. Paragraph 2 covers (a) necessary personal use, (b) official diplomatic/consular/international organisation purposes enjoying immunity, and (c) qualifying publicly funded civil-society/media activities promoting democracy, human rights or rule of law in Belarus. No numerical personal allowance is stated.
2. [VID: Cash](https://www.vid.gov.lv/en/cash). Main-text update displayed: 3 December 2024; form attachments have separate later dates. Explicit reasonable-personal-travel-needs wording, EU-currency scope, declaration at €10,000 or more, actual-carrier example, and controls below threshold where criminal activity is suspected.
3. [Lithuanian Customs: International restrictive measures](https://mano.muitine.lt/en/knowledge-bank/prohibitions-and-restrictions/international-sanctions/international-restrictive-measures), published 15 September 2026. General sanctions implementation, not a €60 allowance. Its Belarus regulation link leads to a 2019 consolidation; that linked edition must not be used for current banknote law.
4. [Lithuanian Customs: Import and export of cash](https://mano.muitine.lt/en/knowledge-bank/travelling-or-third-countries/import-and-export-cash), published 4 May 2026. €10,000-or-more threshold, all currencies combined, actual carrier, origin documents and controls below threshold. Cross-checked against [older English cash guidance](https://muitine.lrv.lt/en/for-passengers/cash-declaration), last updated 12 July 2024. These pages concern declaration, not sanctions export permission.
5. [EUR-Lex: Regulation (EU) 2018/1672](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2018/1672/oj/eng), Articles 2, 3 and 6: cash definition, inclusive declaration threshold, and controls below threshold. Cash includes specified bearer-negotiable instruments and gold as well as currency.
6. [Commission Banknotes FAQ](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/banknotes_en), updated 1 August 2025; [specific PDF inspected](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/document/download/e13ed807-1fcc-4412-8fb4-702d5f05304d_en?filename=faqs-sanctions-russia-euro-banknotes_en.pdf), question 2. Explains a narrow personal-use test, essential/necessary travel and stay needs, excluding commercial/professional/investment uses and delivery to non-travelling acquaintances or relatives. Its detailed discussion and cited judgment concern the analogous **Russian Article 5i**; the article labels this distinction and does not present it as the text of Belarus Article 1za.

## Editorial boundaries

The review date means editorial inspection, not customs endorsement or the sources' effective dates. The unresolved Lithuanian numerical practice is visible in both languages, including the country table and intro. Queue/check-avoidance advice and unrelated goods allowance tables were removed to keep this a cash-specific article. Belarus-side rules are explicitly separate and linked to the State Customs Committee, without importing the EU threshold as a Belarusian rule.

The duplicated cash answer in `src/pages/faq.tsx` was also corrected in RU/EN, including its bullet points. The FAQ structured data is generated from those same answers. A source search across article data, pages, public content and build scripts found no other affirmative copies of the uniform €60 allowance, złoty exemption or “usually not confiscated” advice. Unrelated złoty recommendations concern spending in Poland, not export to Belarus. The separate older Belarus-to-EU customs article needs its own factual audit and is not represented as verified by this work.
